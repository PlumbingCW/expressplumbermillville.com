When a clogged drain is an emergency

Most clogs are a nuisance. A few are not. Water coming up through a floor drain, a basement fixture or a tub when you run the washing machine usually means the blockage is in the main line, and everything you do upstairs adds to the mess.

Treat it as an emergency when sewage is entering the house, when no toilet in the home will flush, or when a single bathroom is the only one you have. Call the 24/7 emergency line, and we will ask the questions that decide which tool goes on the truck.

If a clog is limited to one sink and the rest of the house works, you can usually wait for a same-day appointment when available.

How drain cleaning works, step by step

Every job follows the same logic, even when the equipment changes.

  1. Locate the stoppageWe ask which fixtures are affected, then test water flow to narrow down the branch or main line.
  2. Choose the least aggressive tool that worksA hand tool or small cable comes first for a fixture. Machines and jetting come in when the clog is deeper or has returned.
  3. Clear and confirmWe run water through the line to confirm it flows freely, not just that the cable passed through.
  4. Look for the reasonA clog has a cause. Grease, roots, a belly in the pipe or a flushed item each call for a different follow-up.

Drain snaking and cabling

Snaking is the most common way to open a blocked line. A flexible steel cable with a cutting head spins into the pipe, breaks up the obstruction or hooks it so it can be pulled back. Light cables handle sink and tub branches, while a heavier drum machine reaches farther into a main.

A cable does not clean the pipe wall. It pokes a hole through the blockage, which is why a snaked kitchen drain can slow down again within weeks. If a line has clogged more than once, ask for jetting or a camera pass instead of another round of the same fix.

Broken cable ends and tools left in a pipe are a real problem for later drain work. Tell the technician if a snake or an item is already stuck in the line.

Kitchen sink and laundry drain clogs

Kitchen lines carry grease, starch and food scraps. Those cool into a paste that coats the pipe, then catch every new scrap. A disposal grinds food finer but does not stop fat from sticking farther down.

A washer drain clogs differently. Lint and detergent residue build up in the standpipe, and a backed-up washer that overflows the standpipe can mean the shared branch or main is also partly blocked. If the washer backs up and a nearby floor drain gurgles, treat it as a main line problem.

If your garbage disposal hums and the sink does not drain, the jam may be in the unit, not the pipe. Our garbage disposal repair page covers that case.

Shower and tub drains

Hair and soap scum catch at the top of the drain, usually within reach of a hand tool. Pulling the stopper or strainer and cleaning it is a safe first step you can try yourself.

If water backs up in the tub when a toilet flushes or the washer runs, the blockage is lower in the line and a stopper cleaning will not fix it. Standing water that smells like sewage is a sign to stop using the fixtures and call.

Why chemical drain cleaners are a poor first move

Caustic liquids work on soft organic buildup in a clear pipe. A standing clog gives them nothing to flow through, so they sit, heat up and can soften some plastic fittings or damage older metal traps.

Whoever opens the drain next may be working over a bath of harsh chemical. A cable or hand tool is cleaner, faster and gentler on the plumbing.

Main line clogs and the city's role

When several fixtures back up at once, the blockage is usually in the main drain or the lateral. In Millville, the Sewer Utility asks residents with drain problems to call it first, because its staff check the sewer main and report what they find. If the city main is clear, the problem sits in your lateral, and the city page says the owner maintains and cleans it.

That sequence saves you money. A short call to the city, followed by a plumber if the main is fine, keeps you from paying to clear a line that is not yours to clear. See our sewer line cleaning page for how a main line job runs.

Check your cleanout cap while you are at it. The city's FAQ says a loose or missing cap is the homeowner's job to replace. We also cover Vineland, where a different city utility handles the main.

Drain pipe damage and repeat clogs

Not every clog is a blockage in a healthy pipe. A cracked or sagging section catches solids, and roots enter through joints. In those cases, clearing the line buys time but the clog returns.

A camera look at the line shows which kind of problem you have. If the pipe is damaged, a repair or lining discussion belongs on the table, and a leak under the slab or in the yard may need separate attention.

A drain line that leaks into a wall or ceiling is a different job from a clog. Stop the flow to that fixture and call, and we will sort it out.

Keeping drains clear between visits

Small habits prevent most repeat calls.

  • Wipe greasy pans with a paper towel before washing, and put cooled fat in the trash, not the sink.
  • Use drain strainers in showers and tubs, and empty them before they fill.
  • Flush only toilet paper and human waste, including products labeled flushable.
  • Run hot water for a minute after washing dishes to move residue along.
  • Keep the cleanout cap on and tight, and know where the cleanout is.
  • Schedule a camera check if the same drain has clogged twice in a few months.
