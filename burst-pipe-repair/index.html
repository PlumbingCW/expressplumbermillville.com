When a burst pipe is an emergency: the first ten minutes

Speed matters, but order matters more. These four moves cut the damage while you wait for a plumber.

Stop the water

Turn the main shutoff clockwise until it stops. Open the lowest faucet to drain the lines.

Kill the heat and power

Turn off the water heater. If water is near outlets or the panel, stay away and call from a dry place.

Document everything

Photograph the break, wet areas and damaged items before you clean. Insurance adjusters ask for them.

Contain what you can

Move rugs and boxes, use towels and a wet vac, and open windows to start drying the room.

Which pipe failed matters for the repair

Material shapes how a pipe fails and how we fix it. This table is a general guide, not a diagnosis of your house.

Pipe materialHow it usually failsTypical repair approach
CopperPinholes from corrosion, splits from freezing, loose jointsCut out the section and install new pipe or a press fitting
PEXSplits from freezing, damaged fittings, kinksReplace the section with new tubing and fresh fittings
CPVCBrittle cracks at joints or near heatCut out the cracked portion and re-solvent the joint
Galvanized steelInternal rust, thin walls, leaks at threadsReplace a longer section, or plan a whole-house replacement
Cast iron (drain)Cracks, rotted bottoms, failed jointsReplace the damaged section with no-hub couplings

If your house is older and has more than one failed spot, repeated patches may cost more in total than a larger replacement. Repiping is the usual conversation at that point.

Frozen pipe repair: thaw, inspect, then decide

Water expands as it freezes, which can split the pipe even before it thaws. The break often shows up only after the ice melts, when water starts running behind a wall or under a floor.

If a pipe is frozen, leave the nearest faucet slightly open, shut off the main if you see any leak, and avoid open flame. Once the pipe is thawed, we inspect it for splits, test the line under pressure and replace any damaged section.

Repair types we use after a pipe cracks

The right fix depends on pipe material, access and how widespread the damage is.

  1. Cut and replaceThe damaged section is cut out and new pipe is installed with soldered, pressed or crimped fittings.
  2. Repair couplingA short split can sometimes be sleeved with a repair fitting. Access and pipe condition decide if this is sound.
  3. RerouteIf the wall is hard to open, a new run may go through an attic, closet or exposed section.
  4. Larger replacementWhen the pipe is thin or corroded over a long run, replacing a bigger stretch avoids a second call.

What a repair visit looks like

After the water is shut off, the plumber finds the failed spot and checks nearby pipe for the same weakness. You get a clear price before the pipe is cut, and the repair is pressure tested before the wall or floor is closed.

Drywall, flooring and drying are separate work from the plumbing. We can point you toward next steps, and a leak detection check helps if water appeared in more than one place.

When a broken valve means no water at all

A broken shutoff, a failed supply valve or a snapped fitting under a sink can cut water to a fixture or a whole house. These are often smaller repairs than a split pipe, but they feel urgent because there is no way to isolate the problem.

If water is leaking from a valve you cannot close, shut off the main and call us. Once the line is isolated, we replace the valve and test the connection.

Burst pipes and the city's water

Millville's water comes from city-run wells and a main network of more than 100 miles of pipe, so a break at the street connection is a utility matter. The Water Utility emergency line is 856-825-7000 ext. 7613, and its office hours are Monday to Friday, 7:00 am to 3:30 pm.

Call the utility if water is surging at the meter or the shutoff will not close. For a break inside your walls, the repair is yours to arrange, so call us. What not to do with a burst pipe

A few common reactions make the damage worse.

  • Do not walk into standing water if the panel or outlets are near it.
  • Do not use open flame to thaw a pipe, since it can ignite framing.
  • Do not force a stuck valve with a wrench, since the stem can snap.
  • Do not leave the water heater on after the main is closed.
  • Do not wait for a stain to grow before calling.
  • Do not tear out finished walls until the leak is located.
