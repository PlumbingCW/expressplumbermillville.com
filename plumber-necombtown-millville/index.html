Plumber in Necombtown, Millville, NJ

Necombtown is a residential area on the west side of Millville, built around West Main Street (Route 49) and roads such as Fairton Road and Carmel Road. Neighborhood data sites list it as one of the city's larger residential areas, with a mix of detached houses, attached homes and rentals.

That mix is the practical point for plumbing. A homeowner, a landlord and a tenant often face the same problem with different responsibilities, so this page sorts out who handles what and which questions to ask first.

Questions About Plumbing in Necombtown

Neighborhood sites use Necombtown, while at least one local road and parcel record uses Newcombtown. We treat them as the same place and rely on your street address rather than the spelling.

Data sites estimate many homes there were built before 1940, but age does not tell us what pipe is inside. A camera look at the main drain and a check of supply piping give real answers.

Call the Sewer Utility first so staff can check the main. If the main is clear, the owner is responsible for the lateral and for the cleanout cap.

What housing age data can and cannot tell you

Neighborhood data sites estimate that Necombtown has a large share of homes built before 1940 and many more from the 1950s and 1960s. Those figures come from survey estimates and some of the same pages contain obvious errors, so treat them as a rough guide only.

A house of that age may have had plumbing work over the decades, or it may not. If you are buying or renewing a home, a sewer inspection camera look at the main drain tells you far more than the year on the deed.

Questions to ask when you rent or own an older home

These help an owner, a landlord or a tenant.

  • Where is the main water shutoff, and does anyone on the property have a key or handle for it?
  • Is there a cleanout for the sewer lateral, and is its cap in place?
  • How old is the water heater, and is there a drain pan under it?
  • Has any section of the supply or drain piping been replaced, and is there a record of it?
  • Who pays for repairs: the owner, the landlord under the lease, or the tenant?
  • Have slow drains or damp spots shown up before, and where?

The city's service line questionnaire

Millville's 2026 water report says the city has found no lead service lines. It is still collecting a service line material questionnaire from owners to complete its inventory, and a separate city program page plans a ten-year lead and galvanized service line replacement program.

If your home is in west Millville, ask the Water Utility whether your questionnaire is on file. If you suspect trouble in the pipe from the street to the house, water line repair is the service to ask about, but ask the utility where its responsibility ends first, because the city page does not say.

Route 49 and the towns to the west

West Main Street is State Route 49, which also runs through Bridgeton. If you live near the western edge of the area, our page for a plumber in Bridgeton covers how the city of Bridgeton handles water and sewer, which differs from Millville.

For drains that slow down in a rental or a shared building, drain cleaning can show whether the clog is in a fixture, a branch line or the main. Our Millville plumbing overview covers the rest of the city.

Rentals and shared responsibility

Renter-occupied apartments are common in this area according to neighborhood data. If you rent, report a leak or backup to your landlord in writing and keep copies. If you own, ask whether your tenant knows where the shutoff is.
