What a sewer camera inspection is for

A sewer line is out of sight, so most problems are guessed at until something fails. The camera replaces the guess. It tells you if a backup was a one-time clog or a sign of a damaged pipe.

Inspection also gives you a baseline. A recording today lets you compare the line after a repair, a cleaning or a few years of use.

Gurgling drains and slow backups: what is in the line?

A gurgling toilet, a tub that bubbles when the washer drains, or a slow drain that returns after cleaning all point to something in the main line. It could be grease, roots, a low spot or a broken joint.

A sewer camera inspection answers the question with a picture, so you pay to fix the right thing. It is the branded term for what many people search as sewer line camera, sewer video or looking down the line.

Each finding means something different for your next step.

Not every finding calls for work. A small offset in a line that drains well may only need monitoring. We say plainly when a finding is cosmetic and when it is urgent.

How an inspection visit goes

The visit is short when the line is open.

  1. Find the access pointA cleanout is the easiest entry. Without one, the camera may go in through a toilet flange or roof vent.
  2. Clean if neededIf waste or grease blocks the view, the line is cleared first so the camera can see the pipe wall.
  3. Run the cameraWe record as it travels, noting distances, bends and any defects, and mark the line on the surface if needed.
  4. Explain and reportYou get the recording and written notes, plus a plain explanation of what each finding means.

Using an inspection when you buy or sell

A home inspector usually checks what can be seen. A sewer camera looks at the part nobody sees. For a buyer, the recording can support a price conversation or a repair request. For a seller, a clean report can calm a buyer.

If the property is on septic, the rules differ. A third party septic inspection is required whenever a home is sold, and the inspecting contractor must be registered with the county office. A sewer camera is not a substitute for that inspection, so ask the county what applies to your sale.

For a sale in Millville, the Certificate of Occupancy is applied for through the Fire Department, according to the city page. Ask the city early so the timeline holds.

What a camera cannot tell you

A camera shows the inside surface of the pipe. It cannot measure soil washout around a pipe, and it may not pass a sharp bend, a bad offset or a full collapse. When that happens, the inspection ends there, and the note tells you where.

Murky water, heavy buildup or a belly that holds water can also limit the picture. Cleaning first improves the view. If a result is unclear, we say so and suggest a second look after cleaning, not a guess.

When the inspection leads to repair

If the camera finds damage, the next question is how to fix it. Our sewer line repair page covers spot repair, lining and replacement, with the trade-offs for each.

If the finding is buildup in a sound pipe, cleaning solves it. See drain cleaning for the usual methods. A line that keeps clogging after cleaning is a reason to book a follow-up look.

Local note for Millville and Vineland

In Millville, the Sewer Utility asks residents with drain problems to call it first, since its staff check the sewer main and report their findings. A camera result that shows the blockage is past your property line is useful to bring to that conversation.

Vineland sits just north and has its own septic oversight through the Vineland City Health Department, not the county. If you own in Vineland, check which office handles a septic sale inspection before you book.

Good times to book a camera check

Consider an inspection when any of these apply.

  • Drains are slow in more than one fixture, or a toilet gurgles when another fixture runs.
  • A backup happened and the cause was never confirmed.
  • You are buying a house with older plumbing or large trees near the lateral.
  • A sewer smell lingers in the yard or basement with no obvious source.
  • Before a renovation that adds fixtures or changes the line.
  • After repeat cleanings that did not hold.
