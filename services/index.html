How to choose the right service

Start with what you can see. Water on the floor, a backed-up drain or a cold shower each point to a different service, and the pages above explain the signs and the fixes in plain language.

If two services seem to fit, call anyway. A single symptom such as low water pressure can come from a failing service line, a stuck valve or a leak elsewhere, and a short conversation usually narrows it down faster than guessing.

Emergency and planned work

Some jobs cannot wait. Burst pipes, sewer backups and active leaks go to the front of the line, and the emergency line is open every hour of every day.

Planned jobs such as water heater installation, plumbing installation and repiping are scheduled around you, with the price agreed before work starts.

What to ask any plumber before work starts

These questions help with us or anyone else.

  • Ask for the plumbing license number and check it with the Division of Consumer Affairs.
  • Ask what is causing the problem, in plain words, before you hear about the fix.
  • Ask for the price in writing before work starts, and what would change it.
  • Ask who handles any permit, and check permit rules with the Millville Construction Office.
  • Ask what to watch for afterward and whether any cleanup falls to you or another trade.

What to do if your problem is not listed

Plumbing covers more ground than any list can. If you have a problem that does not match a page above, such as a noisy pipe, a smell with no source or a fixture that behaves oddly, call and describe it. We will say plainly whether it is something we handle.

Some work belongs to other trades. Foundation repair, structural drying and most electrical work fall outside plumbing, and heating system work may need separate licensing. When that is the case we tell you and, where it helps, explain what to ask the next contractor.
