Frozen Pipe Repair in Millville, NJ: What to Do Before It Bursts

By the Millville Express Plumbing team · Published October 23, 2026

If a faucet gives you a trickle or nothing at all in freezing weather, a pipe is probably frozen. Open that faucet, find the cold section of pipe, and warm it slowly with a hair dryer or warm towels. Never use a torch or any open flame, and shut off the main water valve if you see water, a bulge or a split.

A frozen pipe is a blockage made of ice. A burst pipe is a hole. The two can look alike at first, which is why the order of your first few moves matters more than speed.

Frozen or burst: how to tell the difference

Frozen pipes often stay intact until the ice melts. Many splits only show up once the water starts moving again, so check before you thaw anything.

If you are unsure which row fits, treat it as the more serious one. Shutting off the water costs you nothing, and a split pipe left running does damage by the minute.

How to thaw a frozen pipe safely

Work in this order. Every step is meant to keep a small problem from becoming a flooded room.

  1. Open the faucetTurn on the tap served by the frozen pipe. Moving water and a path for steam to escape help the ice clear as it melts.
  2. Find the cold sectionRun your hand along the pipe from the faucet back toward the wall or floor. The spot that feels coldest, or shows frost, is usually where the ice sits.
  3. Apply gentle heatUse a hair dryer on low, a heating pad, or towels soaked in warm water. Start nearest the faucet and work back toward the blockage.
  4. Keep the water off the electricsDo not stand in water while using any plug-in heater or dryer. If a wet outlet or cord is in reach, do not touch it and shut off power at the panel if you can do so safely.
  5. Check for leaks as flow returnsWatch the pipe, the ceiling below it and the floor around it. If water appears where it should not, close the main valve and call.

What never to use on a frozen pipe

Skip propane torches, blowtorches, kerosene heaters, charcoal and anything with an open flame. Pipe sits against wood framing and insulation, and a flame near either can start a fire you will not see until later.

Also skip boiling water poured over pipe, and do not run a space heater unattended in a crawlspace. Slow, even warmth is safer for the pipe and the building.

Where pipes tend to freeze in a house

Freezing follows cold air, not pipe size. Lines inside exterior walls, runs along a rim joist, pipes in a crawlspace, and anything in an unheated garage, porch or utility room are the usual suspects. Outdoor hose bibs and the short pipe feeding them are especially exposed.

Kitchen and bathroom sink lines on an outside wall deserve a look as well, because cabinet doors keep warm room air away from them. Opening those doors on a hard-freeze night lets heat reach the pipe.

Pipes that have frozen once often freeze again in the same place. If the same faucet quits every cold snap, the cause is the pipe route or missing insulation, and that is worth fixing before the next freeze.

Steps to take before a cold snap

A short walk-through a day or two ahead is cheaper than any repair. Do these while the weather is still mild enough to work comfortably.

  • Disconnect garden hoses and, where your setup allows, shut off and drain the supply to outdoor faucets.
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls so room heat reaches the pipes.
  • Let a thin stream of cold water run overnight at the tap farthest from where water enters the house.
  • Add foam pipe sleeves to exposed lines in crawlspaces, garages and basements.
  • Seal drafts around pipe entry points, rim joists and vents with caulk or spray foam.
  • Keep the thermostat steady, even if you are away, rather than setting it very low.
  • Know exactly where your main shutoff valve is and that it turns without a fight.

After the ice clears: check before you relax

A pipe can hold while frozen and leak once thawed. Leave the faucet open a few minutes, then close it and look at the pipe, the joints and the surfaces around and below it. Check again an hour later.

Look for stains on ceilings, a musty smell, or a water meter that keeps moving when everything is off. Those point to a hidden split, and our burst pipe repair service covers finding and fixing it.

If water arrives at the house from the street and the ice is out in the yard rather than in the walls, the problem sits in the line underground. That falls under water line repair, not a quick thaw at the faucet.

If there is no water at all in Millville

Millville's water comes from ten city wells, and the city runs the Water Utility, which maintains the mains under the street. If a whole block has lost water, the issue may be on the city side, so a call to the utility is worth making before you start taking pipes apart. Its emergency line is 856-825-7000 ext. 7613, and its office is open Monday to Friday, 7:00 am to 3:30 pm.

The pipe from the main to your house is called the service pipe. The city page does not spell out where its responsibility ends and yours begins, so ask the Water Utility directly. That answer decides whether a frozen or split line near the street is a call to the city or to a plumber.

When to stop and call a plumber

Call when you cannot find the frozen spot, when it sits behind a finished wall, or when the pipe is already bulged or cracked. Call if thawing does nothing after a reasonable try, or if you see any water where it should not be.
