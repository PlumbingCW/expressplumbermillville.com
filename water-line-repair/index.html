When a service line leak is an emergency

A service line leak is an emergency if water is surfacing fast, the yard is eroding, the house has lost pressure or the meter is spinning with everything closed. Water in the soil can undermine a driveway, walkway or foundation edge over time.

Close the main valve inside the house first. If the valve will not close or the water is clearly on the street side, call the Millville Water Utility emergency line at 856-825-7000 ext. 7613, then call us.

Low pressure, wet yard or a meter that will not stop?

These three symptoms often share one cause: a damaged line between the street and the house. Low pressure comes from water escaping before it reaches your taps. A wet yard is that water surfacing. A meter that will not stop is the meter counting the loss.

The branded term for the fix is water service line repair, and it starts with finding where the leak is. A leak detection visit can place the break before anyone digs.

How we find the problem on the line

The goal is to dig once, in the right place.

  1. Check the basicsConfirm the main valve is fully open and the pressure regulator is working. A partly closed valve can mimic a leak.
  2. Run a meter testWith everything off, read the meter, wait, and read again. Any movement means water is leaving the system.
  3. Isolate the lineClose the house main to see whether the meter still moves. If it does, the leak is on the service line.
  4. Listen and markAcoustic listening on the exposed pipe ends and along the route narrows the leak to a short stretch.
  5. Choose the repairWe explain the spot repair, section replacement or full replacement, and the price comes before any digging.

Trench or trenchless: what changes

A trench exposes the full pipe, which makes it easy to inspect and replace, but it removes lawn, walkways or driveway along the route. Trenchless work uses entry and exit pits and pulls the new pipe through the old path.

Trenchless is not always possible. Tight bends, shallow depth, other utilities or a collapsed old pipe may force open digging. Ask which method fits your yard and what restoration is included in the scope.

Low pressure is not always the service line

Low pressure across the whole house can come from a partly closed main valve, a failing pressure regulator, clogged interior pipes or a leak on the service line. One weak faucet usually points to a clogged aerator or cartridge instead.

If your house has older steel piping, the narrowing inside can cut flow even when the service line is fine. In that case, repiping may be a better answer than digging the yard.

Wet spot beside the meter box

Water near the meter box can come from the utility's side, the fitting on your side or the line past it. A quick look at which side of the meter is wet helps decide who should be called.

Do not dig around the meter yourself. Contact the Water Utility if the leak is at the meter, and call us if it follows the pipe toward the house.

Before the plumber arrives

A little preparation speeds up the visit.

  • Locate the main shutoff inside the house and confirm it closes fully.
  • Write down the meter reading and the time, then check again after a short wait.
  • Note where the yard is wet, soft or sinking, and keep children away from it.
  • Clear the path to the meter box and the house entry point of the line.
  • Photograph the wet area for insurance and for the plumber.
  • Tell us if the line has been repaired before, and where.

When the break involves pipes inside

If water also appears inside the home, a split in a branch line may be the cause. Burst pipe repair covers that situation, and the two problems can happen together.
