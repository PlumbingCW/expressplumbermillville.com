Common Questions About Faucet Repair in Millville

Yes. The emergency line is open 24/7 for faucets and valves that will not shut off, broken supply lines and fixtures that are flooding a room. For a plain drip we offer same-day appointments when available. We do not promise an arrival time, and we tell you the price before we start.

Repair makes sense when the body is sound and a cartridge, washer or seat has worn out. Replacement is better when the finish is pitted, the body is cracked, or parts for that model are hard to find. A faucet that has been repaired more than once is usually a replacement candidate.

A kit works if you match the brand and model exactly and can shut off the supply. It will not fix corrosion around the valve seat or a cracked body. If the drip returns after the kit, stop and call before parts get damaged.

A tub or shower valve usually has a cartridge or stem that has worn out behind the wall. The drip may continue because the seal no longer closes. Fixing it means removing the trim and sometimes opening access to the valve body.

Do not force it. Old stop valves can crack or break off when pushed. Close the main valve for the house and call us, because the valve may need to be replaced before the faucet can be repaired.

A leak at the showerhead is often a worn washer or loose connection. A leak behind the handle points to the cartridge. Water that stains the ceiling below suggests a leak in the wall, which needs a different kind of look.

A drip from the spout is usually a worn washer. Water that leaks inside the wall when the spigot is on is more serious. Close the inside shutoff for that line if you can find it, and call us.

Scale is a smaller factor here. The city's 2026 water quality report lists carbonate hardness of 29 ppm, which is soft. Faucets still wear from use, so aerators and cartridges do fail, but mineral crusting is less common than in hard water areas.

Drip, spray or flood: how urgent is it?

A faucet that drips once a second is not an emergency, but it still wastes water and wears the seat faster the longer it runs. A faucet that will not turn off, a stop valve that is leaking when touched, or a supply line spraying under the sink is different. Those cases call for the main valve and a phone call.

If water is soaking cabinets or flooring, move items out of the way and keep towels under the valve. Water that reaches electrical outlets or a power strip means you should avoid touching them and call us.

While you wait for us

These steps are safe for any homeowner and protect your floors and cabinets.

Find the right valve

Look under the sink for two small oval handled valves. Turn each clockwise until snug. If there are none, use the main water shutoff.

Open the faucet

Once the valve is closed, open the faucet to drain pressure. That stops the flow and shows whether the valve is holding.

Protect the area

Put a bucket or towels under leaking lines and move stored items out of the cabinet.

Keep power away

If water reaches outlets, cords or appliances, do not touch them. Call us and tell us what is wet.

Shutoff valves and supply lines

Under-sink stop valves and braided supply lines fail more often than the faucet itself, usually from age or from being bumped. A burst line puts a full stream of water in the cabinet, which is why a working shutoff valve matters. Test yours once or twice a year by turning it off and on.

When a supply line has burst behind a wall or under flooring, the situation moves beyond fixture work. We would then treat it as burst pipe repair, and we would check the line before we put a new faucet back on it.

Tubs, showers and the leak you cannot see

A dripping tub spout is easy to spot. A leak inside the wall behind the handle is not, and it can stain a ceiling in the room below before you notice a drip at all. A damp patch or soft drywall near a shower is a reason to call, even if the faucet looks fine.

When we cannot see where the water is going, we use leak detection methods to trace the source before opening any walls. That keeps repairs to the part that is actually leaking.

Signs a faucet repair has become a replacement

One or two of these can be repaired. Three or more usually means a new faucet is the better buy.

  • The finish is peeling, pitted or corroded around the base or spout.
  • The handle is loose and tightening it does not fix the wobble.
  • Replacement cartridges for your model are no longer made.
  • The faucet has already been repaired for the same drip once or twice.
  • Water seeps from the body of the faucet, not just from the spout.
  • You want a different type, such as a pull down kitchen faucet.

Replacing a faucet

If a faucet is past repair, replacement is straightforward when the sink and supply valves are in good shape. Moving the faucet or changing the number of holes in the countertop is a bigger change, and we cover that under plumbing installation.

Bring the model number or a photo of the old faucet to the call. It helps us confirm that the new one fits the sink holes.

Pricing

What changes the price

Every job gets a clear price before work starts. These are the things that move it up or down.

A cartridge swap and a new faucet are different jobs. A new fixture adds material cost on top of labor.

Tub and shower valves usually need wall access, while a sink faucet is open under the counter.

A stuck or corroded shutoff valve may need replacing before the faucet work can start.

Older or specialty models may need ordered parts, which can turn a repair into a replacement.

After-hours emergency dispatch is priced differently from a same-day appointment.

Insurance note. A supply line that bursts suddenly may fall under water damage coverage, but a worn washer or the cost of the faucet itself usually does not. Check your policy language or call your agent.
