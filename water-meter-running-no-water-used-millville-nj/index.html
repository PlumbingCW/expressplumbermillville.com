Water Meter Running With Nothing On in Millville, NJ

By the Millville Express Plumbing team · Published October 23, 2026

If your water meter keeps moving when every tap, appliance and toilet in the house is idle, water is going somewhere. The most common causes are a leaking toilet flapper, an irrigation zone, a dripping fixture, or a leak in the buried pipe between the street and the house.

You can narrow it down yourself with a simple meter test. For Millville, NJ homeowners, the test also tells you whether to call a plumber or the Water Utility first, which can save a lot of guessing.

Run the meter test

Do this when nobody is home or everyone is willing to keep the water off for a while.

  1. Shut everything offClose all taps, make sure the dishwasher and washing machine are not running, and turn off the ice maker if you can. Do not flush.
  2. Find the meterIt is usually in a box near the street, in the basement, or in a utility closet. Lift the lid carefully and clear away dirt or debris.
  3. Look at the low-flow indicatorMany meters have a small dial or triangle that spins with even a trickle. If it moves with everything off, water is flowing.
  4. Take two readingsIf there is no indicator, write down the numbers. Wait without using any water, then read again. Any change means a leak.
  5. Close the main shutoff and repeatShut the valve where the line enters the house, then check the meter. If it still moves, the leak is before that valve.

What the result tells you

The shutoff step is the one that splits the problem in half.

Meter resultWhere the leak isNext step
Moves with all fixtures off, stops when the house valve is closedInside the house or yard plumbingCheck toilets, irrigation, heater and fixtures
Keeps moving even with the house valve closedThe pipe between the meter and the houseCall for leak detection and line repair
Moves only in short burstsA toilet filling after a slow flapper leak, or an ice makerDye test the toilets, check appliances
Moves steadily with no pauseA running fixture or a larger leakShut the main and call right away
Does not move at allNo active leak at the momentCheck billing and meter reading history

A steady spin with the house valve closed is the strongest sign of a buried line problem, and it is worth acting on quickly.

The usual suspects inside the house

Check these in order. Most hidden usage comes from the first two.

  • Toilets: add a few drops of food coloring to each tank and look in the bowl after fifteen minutes for color.
  • Irrigation: look for a zone stuck on, a cracked head, or a valve that does not close fully.
  • Water heater: look for dampness at the relief valve and at the bottom of the tank.
  • Ice maker and filters: a loose fitting behind a fridge can drip for weeks without being noticed.
  • Softener or filter: a stuck valve can keep water flowing to drain, if you have one.
  • Outdoor hose bibs: look for water at the base of the wall and for a hose left connected.

Why the toilet is the top culprit

A flapper that no longer seals lets water trickle from the tank into the bowl all day. The fill valve then tops up the tank every few minutes, so the meter moves while the toilet looks quiet. You can often hear a faint refill hiss in a silent house.

The dye test finds this in minutes. A worn flapper is a small part to replace, and the fill valve is worth checking at the same time if it keeps running.

When the leak is underground

If the meter spins with the house valve closed, the pipe between the meter and your home is the suspect. Signs include a soft, green or wet patch in the yard, a drop in water pressure, or the sound of water near the meter box.

Do not dig to chase it. Locating a buried leak with the right equipment limits how much ground is opened, and our leak detection work is built for that. Once found, water line repair fixes the pipe itself.

Your meter and the Water Utility

Millville's water system is run by the City Water Utility under Public Works, which draws from ten wells and serves more than 100 miles of mains. The utility's office is open weekdays from 7:00 am to 3:30 pm, and its emergency line is 856-825-7000 ext. 7613. Call it if water is running at the meter or street side, and ask where the city's responsibility ends and yours begins, since the city page does not state the split.

The city also notes that lead can enter water from service pipes between the main and the home. The city is also collecting a service line material questionnaire from owners, so ask the utility whether it needs yours. The utility says its water meets federal and state drinking water standards.

Reading a high bill

A high bill with no change in habits is a good reason to run the test above. Compare the bill period to the one before, and ask the utility how the reading was taken. A slow constant leak, such as a toilet, can quietly add up over a billing cycle.

If your home uses a private well, there is no utility meter to read, and you would check the pressure tank and pump instead. Leaks still waste power in that case.
