A sewer backup that cannot wait

A main line backup can move quickly from slow to flooding. Here is what to do before help arrives.

Stop all water use

No toilets, showers, dishwasher or washing machine. Water you add has to go somewhere, and the lowest drain takes it.

Keep people and pets away

Wastewater carries germs. Avoid contact, and do not walk through it barefoot.

Do not use a plunger on the tub

Pressure can push contaminated water back toward other fixtures. Leave the line alone.

Find your cleanout

Locating the capped opening before the technician arrives saves time. Do not open it if wastewater is pressing against it.

How main line cleaning is done

The work follows the line from the house toward the street.

  1. Open the cleanoutThe cap comes off carefully. If wastewater is under pressure, the opening is handled slowly to avoid a spill.
  2. Cable the lineA heavy cable machine with a cutting head breaks up the blockage and pulls debris back.
  3. Clean to the streetThe cable runs as far as the lateral goes. The city says cleaning must pass the curb to the center of the street.
  4. Flush and verifyWater is run to confirm flow, and a camera look is offered if the cause is still unclear.

What clogs a main line

Roots are the usual suspect in a line with joints or cracks. They push in for moisture, spread into a mat and trap everything that follows. Grease is another common cause, especially from kitchens, since it cools and coats the pipe wall.

Wipes, paper towels, feminine products and dental floss do not break down. They tangle on roots and rough spots and form a plug that a toilet cannot push through.

A low spot in the pipe, called a belly, lets solids settle. Cleaning clears it, but the problem returns until the pipe is corrected.

When cleaning is not enough

If the line backs up again within weeks, if the cable keeps hitting a hard stop, or if the cleaning brings up pieces of pipe, the issue is the pipe itself. A camera look at the line shows whether it is cracked, offset or collapsed.

From there, the decision moves to sewer line repair. A sewer inspection is the bridge between the two.

Local note for the Millville and Bridgeton area

Millville's sewer treatment plant sits on the Maurice River at Fowser Road, and the Sewer Utility keeps staff on call 24/7. That means you can reach a person at the city when the main might be the problem, and it is a better first call than guessing whether the blockage is yours. Sewage overflow from an un-maintained lateral is reported to the Housing and Inspection Department, so clearing it fast matters.

If your property is in Bridgeton, water and sewer there run through the City of Bridgeton Division of Water and Sewer instead. Call that office to learn where the city line begins.

Keeping the main line clear

Most main line backups are slow-building. These habits help.

  • Flush only toilet paper and human waste. Wipes of every kind go in the trash.
  • Scrape cooking fat into a container and throw it away instead of washing it down.
  • Know where the cleanout is and keep the cap on tight. The city says replacing a missing cap is the homeowner's job.
  • Watch for slow drains in more than one fixture. That is the early sign of a main line problem.
  • Keep large trees and shrubs from growing over the lateral when you plant.
  • Book a camera look if the line has backed up twice in a short time.
