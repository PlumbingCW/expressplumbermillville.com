No heat: what we check

Start with the basics, which you can check safely, then call if the system still does not run.

  1. Check the thermostatMake sure it is set to heat, set above room temperature, and that the batteries are fresh.
  2. Look at the powerConfirm the boiler switch is on and the breaker has not tripped. Reset a breaker once only.
  3. Read the displayNote any error code or flashing light and look it up in the manual. Tell us what it says when you call.
  4. Check pressure and fuelA low pressure reading or an empty fuel tank can stop a boiler. Do not add water or relight a pilot if you are unsure.

Pressure, the expansion tank and leaks

A hot water boiler is a closed loop of water at a set pressure. When the water heats, it expands, and the expansion tank absorbs that extra volume. A tank that has lost its air charge or failed will let pressure spike, which can open the relief valve and drip water.

A leak on a fitting or valve is usually a repair. A leak that traces back to the boiler body or heat exchanger usually points to replacement. If you cannot tell where water is coming from, a leak detection visit can trace it before the damage spreads.

Circulator pumps and zone valves

The circulator pump moves hot water through the pipes to baseboards, radiators or in-floor loops. When it weakens or fails, the boiler may fire but the rooms stay cold. You may hear a hum or whine, or feel a pump that is hot but not moving water.

Zone valves open and close to send heat to different areas of the house. A stuck valve can leave one zone cold while the others are fine. Both parts are usually replaceable without replacing the whole boiler.

Repair or replace the boiler?

Repair is the sensible choice when one component has failed, the boiler is otherwise sound, and parts are available. Pumps, valves, thermostats and expansion tanks often fit that case.

Replacement deserves a hard look when the heat exchanger leaks, the unit has needed repeated repairs, parts are hard to find, or it is badly corroded. If you move to replacement, our plumbing installation page covers the piping and connection work that comes with a new unit. We give you both prices so the decision is yours.

Signs a boiler needs attention

Look for these between heating seasons, not in the middle of a cold night.

  • Rust, white crust or drips around valves, fittings and the base of the boiler.
  • Pressure that sits outside the range printed in your manual.
  • Rooms that heat unevenly, or radiators that stay cold at the top.
  • New banging, whining or gurgling sounds when the boiler runs.
  • A yellow or flickering flame, or soot around the boiler or flue.
  • A boiler that cycles on and off often or takes a long time to warm the house.

Pricing

What changes the price

Every job gets a clear price before work starts. These are the things that move it up or down.

A thermostat or valve costs far less to fix than a circulator, and a heat exchanger leak changes the whole decision.

Older units can have harder-to-find parts, which affects both the repair price and the replace question.

More zones means more valves and more time to trace a cold area.

The fuel type and the condition of the flue or vent can add safety checks and extra work.

A boiler in a tight space, or a call outside normal daytime hours, can change time and equipment.

Insurance note. Policies usually pay for sudden accidental damage, such as water from a burst pipe, but not for wear or the failed boiler part itself. Check your policy or ask your agent if a leak caused damage.
