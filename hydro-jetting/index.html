What hydro jetting actually does

A jetting machine pumps water through a hose that ends in a nozzle with rear-facing jets. The jets pull the hose forward and spray the pipe wall in every direction. Grease softens, scale peels off, and loose debris flushes toward the main.

A cable cleaner pushes through a clog. A jet cleans the pipe as it travels. That difference explains why the same drain can stay open for a year after jetting and clog again in a month after a snake.

Water pressure is a setting, not a constant. A trained operator picks the pressure and nozzle for the pipe in front of them. Cast iron, clay and PVC each behave differently.

When a backed-up line is an emergency

Sewage rising in a floor drain, a toilet that will not flush and water pooling at the lowest fixture all mean the line is blocked past the point where waiting helps. Stop running water and call the 24/7 emergency line, because every flush or load of laundry adds to the overflow.

We will ask which fixtures are affected, because that tells us whether the blockage sits in a branch or the main. That answer decides whether jetting is the right first move or whether a camera look should come first.

Snaked it and it clogged again?

A line that opens up and then backs up again a few weeks later still has buildup on the wall. The cable made a hole in the blockage, and the leftover grease or roots caught the next batch of waste.

That pattern is the best sign that hydro jetting is the better next step. We confirm with a camera when we can, then choose between jetting, a repair, or a maintenance schedule.

When not to jet

Jetting is the wrong tool for a collapsed pipe, a large offset joint or a pipe that is mostly rust flake. The water has nowhere to go, and pressure can force debris through a weak spot.

Fragile or unknown pipe is the reason we look first. If a camera cannot pass because of a collapse, no cleaning method will help and the job moves to repair.

Jetting is also unnecessary for a plain fixture clog. A trap or short branch is a job for a hand tool, and our drain cleaning page covers it.

How a jetting visit runs

The order matters because it keeps a risky pass from happening on a weak pipe.

  1. Find the access pointA cleanout is best. Without one, the line may be reached through a toilet, roof vent or other opening.
  2. Run a camera when it makes senseA quick look shows the pipe material, the buildup and any damage before pressure goes in.
  3. Jet in passesThe nozzle moves forward and back through the line, cleaning from the access point toward the street.
  4. Re-check and reportWe confirm flow and explain what we saw, including anything that needs follow-up repair.

Grease traps and interceptors need their own cleaning. Jetting the line downstream does not replace that.

What a sewer backup means for the rest of your day

A backup is one of the few plumbing problems that is a health issue as well as a repair issue.

Stop adding water

No flushing, laundry, dishwasher or long showers. Every gallon has nowhere to go but the lowest drain.

Keep people away

Sewage on a floor carries bacteria. Keep children and pets out of the area and avoid touching it with bare hands.

Do not use chemical cleaners

They do not cut through a main line clog and can burn skin during cleanup.

Call the 24/7 line

Tell the dispatcher which fixtures are backing up and which is the lowest one affected.
