  1. Look at your billsA sewer charge on a city bill suggests a connection to the Sewer Utility. No sewer charge points toward a septic system.
  2. Call the Sewer UtilityUse the City Hall line, 856-825-7000, and ask the Sewer Utility whether your address is connected.
  3. Ask the county if it is septicThe Cumberland County Department of Health, 856-327-7602, handles septic applications and complaints for Millville.
  4. Look for a cleanout or a tank lidA cleanout near the foundation suggests a lateral to a main. A buried lid in the yard suggests a tank.

Water heaters and water quality near the lake

The Water Utility draws only from city wells, and its 2026 report lists soft water. For a homeowner that means mineral scale is a smaller factor in how long a tank lasts, so age and condition matter more. If your tank is old, leaking or slow to recover, compare water heater repair with water heater replacement before you decide.

If you use a private well instead of city water, the state has well rules and testing guidance. Ask the county health department what applies before you add or replace any treatment equipment.

If your home sits low or near water

Homes near a lake can have a basement or crawlspace, and a sump pump is one common way to move water out of a pit. We have no data that homes here take on more water than elsewhere, so treat this as general guidance. If a pump runs often, cycles at odd times or has stopped, sump pump repair is the next step to ask about.

A pump that runs constantly can also point to a supply leak or a high water bill. A leak detection visit can separate groundwater from a pipe problem before you pay to dig.

Before you buy or sell near the lake

A septic system needs a third-party inspection whenever a home is sold, and city sewer homes have their own checks. Ask the city where your Certificate of Occupancy paperwork stands and ask the county about any septic inspection. We can help you read a plumbing question, but the paperwork itself comes from the city and the county.
