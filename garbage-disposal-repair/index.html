When a garbage disposal problem is an emergency: make it safe first

A disposal can injure fingers and shock you if water reaches the wiring. Do these steps before anything else.

Switch it off

Turn off the wall switch and, if you can reach it, unplug the unit under the sink. Never reach into the drain.

Stop the running water

Turn off the faucet and, if water is escaping the cabinet, close the valves under the sink.

Do not use chemical cleaners

Drain chemicals sit in the bowl and burn skin when a plumber opens the unit. Skip them.

Check for wet electrics

If the plug or outlet is wet, do not touch it. Turn off power at the panel if it is safe to reach.

When a clog is not the disposal's fault

Many calls begin as a disposal problem and turn out to be a drain problem. If the water rises in both sink basins, or the dishwasher stops draining, the clog is down the line. A new disposal will not cure that.

We check the trap and the drain arm first, then use cable tools to clear the blockage. If the blockage is deeper, we move to the same methods as drain cleaning, and we tell you before the scope changes.

Installing or replacing a disposal

A new disposal is a good swap when the sink flange and plumbing underneath are sound. Horsepower matters less than most buyers think: a smaller motor handles ordinary scraps, and a larger one runs quieter and jams less. Continuous feed units run when you switch them on, while batch feed units only run with a stopper in place.

If your kitchen has no disposal today, installing one needs an electrical supply and a switch. That electrical part is separate from the plumbing. We handle the drain and sink connections, which are part of ordinary plumbing installation work.

Repair or replace: a decision rule

Repair when the unit is fairly new and the problem is a jam, a seal or a hose. Replace when the housing leaks, the motor is dead, or the repair would cost close to a new unit. Age alone is not a reason, but a unit that has failed twice already is.

Reconnecting the plumbing after a sink change

New countertops and sink swaps often leave the disposal, trap and dishwasher hose misaligned. A drain that was straight on the old sink may now need new pipe to meet the outlet. We rebuild the trap, reconnect the disposal and dishwasher drain, and test for leaks before we leave.

If the sink faucet also drips or the stop valves are stuck, we can look at those at the same visit. Our faucet repair service covers the fixture side.

What to keep out of the drain

These habits prevent most disposal and kitchen drain calls.

  • Wipe grease and oil from pans into the trash, not the sink.
  • Run cold water before, during and for a short time after grinding.
  • Cut large scraps and avoid stringy items like celery and corn husks.
  • Skip pasta, rice and potato peels, which swell and form a paste.
  • Never put in glass, plastic, metal or bones, which can jam or damage the blades.
  • Do not use hot water to chase grease. It melts, then hardens again further down the line.

What changes the price

Every job gets a clear price before work starts. These are the things that move it up or down.

A jam or hose fix is a short visit. A new unit adds material cost and time to reconnect everything.

Horsepower, feed type and noise insulation change what a replacement costs.

A blocked trap or branch line adds drain work to the disposal work.

A missing switch or outlet is outside plumbing and may need an electrician.

After-hours dispatch is priced differently from a same-day appointment.
