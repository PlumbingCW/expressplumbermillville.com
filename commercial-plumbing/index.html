Before we arrive at your business

These steps limit damage and protect staff and customers.

Stop the water

Close the nearest fixture shutoff or the building main. Tell staff where it is for next time.

Clear the area

Keep people out of wet or sewage affected areas. Wet floors cause falls, and sewage is a health risk.

Protect stock and records

Move boxes, paper and equipment off the floor. Water spreads under shelves faster than it looks.

Stay away from electrics

If water reaches outlets, panels or equipment, do not touch them. Call us and tell us what is wet.

Drains, floor drains and restaurant lines

Commercial drains carry more than home drains do. Restaurant lines collect grease and food waste, and shop floor drains collect dirt, rags and silt. A blocked floor drain can flood a back room in minutes.

We clear these with cable machines and, where the line needs more than a cable can do, hydro jetting. We tell you which method fits your pipe and the blockage before we start. Frequent grease clogs call for a cleaning schedule, not another emergency visit.

Grease traps and the city ordinance

Millville has a grease and oil ordinance meant to prevent blockages from fats, oils and grease, with food businesses as the main target. A trap that is not cleaned on a schedule lets grease escape into your lateral and the city line beyond it. Ask the city what the ordinance requires of your kitchen.

Trap cleaning is planned work. We book it, agree on how often it needs repeating, and avoid the overnight call that comes when the trap overflows on a Saturday night service.

Backflow testing and other scheduled work

A backflow assembly protects the public water supply from contamination if pressure drops. Many commercial buildings and irrigation systems have one. A test shows whether the assembly still works, and a failed test means it needs repair or replacement.

Your water provider tells you whether testing is required and how often. Once you know, call us to book it in advance, because a missed deadline can be more expensive than the test itself.

Leaks in commercial buildings: what is realistic

Finding a hidden leak depends on where the pipe runs. A leak in an accessible mechanical room is simple to trace, while one under a slab or inside a finished wall takes more time and may not be worth locating if the cost exceeds the repair.

We tell you what we expect before booking a leak search. If tracing is unlikely to help, we say so and suggest options such as replacing a section of line.

Coverage along North High Street and Wheaton Avenue

Millville businesses cluster along North High Street, the downtown commercial corridor, and Wheaton Avenue, a commercial and industrial corridor served by Route 55 Exit 26. We dispatch from Millville, so both corridors are inside our regular service area.

Downtown buildings and industrial units have different plumbing needs. We ask about the building type on the first call so we send the right tools.

Working with property managers and landlords

Clear information up front helps tenants and keeps approval simple.

  • Give us the building address, unit number and a contact who can let us in.
  • Tell us who can approve the price, and whether the owner needs to sign off.
  • Send photos of the problem and the nearest shutoff when you can.
  • Share any past work on the same line so we do not repeat a fix that failed.
  • Ask us to separate emergency repairs from items that can be scheduled.
  • Keep tenants informed about water shutoffs and expected quiet hours.
