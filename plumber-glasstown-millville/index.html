Glasstown Arts District

Plumber in the Glasstown Arts District, Millville, NJ

The Glasstown Arts District is the arts district in downtown Millville, with galleries, studios, restaurants, shops and the Levoy Theatre around North High Street. Homes and businesses share these blocks, so a plumbing problem can start in a kitchen, a studio or an apartment and still end up in the same drain.

This page covers what we could confirm about the district, who is responsible for which part of the plumbing, and what to check before you call. Help here starts by phone, so describe what you see and we will talk through the next step.

Where the district sits

The district runs through downtown Millville along North High Street, the downtown commercial corridor. The Levoy Theatre, which reopened in 2012, is listed among its attractions, and tourism sources also mention a river walk along the Maurice River.

The Levoy dates to 1908, so the area clearly mixes older buildings with newer ones. In practice that means a shutoff valve or drain you find in one unit may not serve the next, so locate your own before anything leaks.

What to check before you call about a downtown building

A few minutes of looking makes the phone call far more useful.

  • Find the water shutoff for your unit and ask the owner whether it also controls neighboring units.
  • Note which fixtures are affected: one sink, a whole floor or every drain in the building.
  • Check whether a floor drain or cleanout is backing up, since that points to the line outside.
  • Look at the ceiling below you for stains, which can mean the leak starts in an upstairs unit.
  • Keep clear of electrical items near water, and shut off the supply to a heater if a leak is close by.
  • Photograph any damage before cleanup, because a landlord or insurer may ask for it.
  • Ask whether your lease or the building owner handles plumbing repairs before you hire anyone.

Restaurants, kitchens and the grease question

Millville has a grease and oil ordinance aimed at fats, oils and grease, especially from food businesses. A restaurant or café in the district should ask the city what it requires for its kitchen, because the answer can affect traps and how often drains get serviced.

When a kitchen drain slows down, routine drain cleaning can clear it. Hydro jetting is the option to ask about when grease has built up along the pipe wall, and repeated slow drains are a reason to look at the line before the next full backup.

Who handles what when a drain backs up

The city's own advice is to call the Sewer Utility first for a drain problem, because its staff check the sewer main and report what they find. The main line is 856-825-7000, and you ask for the Sewer Utility. If the main is clear, the trouble is on the property side.

The owner is responsible for the sewer lateral and for a missing or loose cleanout cap. The city's FAQ adds that a plumber cleaning a sewer line must go past the curb to the center of the street, which is why heavy jobs involve more than the pipe you can see. A sewer inspection shows what is wrong before anyone digs.

Water service and the city

Millville's water comes from city wells, and the Water Utility maintains the mains. The city page does not spell out where its responsibility ends and the owner's begins, so ask the utility before you assume a leak near the street is yours to fix. Its emergency line is 856-825-7000 ext. 7613.

The city's 2026 water quality report lists soft water. Mineral scale is therefore a smaller worry for heaters and fixtures here than in hard-water areas, and a leak is more likely to be a worn part than a scaled one.

Jobs that fit a mixed downtown

Shops, studios and offices that share walls with apartments often need commercial plumbing help for floor drains, restrooms and water supply. A stain or a damp smell that you cannot trace is a good case for leak detection before opening any wall.
